\begin{keyidea}[read this before quoting any number in Part III]
Everything in Chapters~\ref{ch:results} to~\ref{ch:findings} describes the repository \emph{as it stood at commit \code{fa5164b}}. After the analysis was finished, the findings were acted on. This appendix records what changed, what the corrected measurements are, and which findings remain open, so that no reader quotes a number this project has already retracted.

\section{The correction that matters most}
\label{sec:fix-abstention}

F1 and F2 were confirmed by direct measurement against the live endpoint rather than by reading the code \tagM:

\begin{measured}[the judge, at three output budgets, same question and answer]
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rlll@{}}
\toprule
\code{max_tokens} & returned content & \code{finish_reason} & read as\\
\midrule
5 & \code{'REF'} & \code{length} & not a refusal\\
16 & \code{'REFUSED'} & \code{stop} & a refusal\\
50 & \code{'REFUSED'} & \code{stop} & a refusal\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The shipped value was 5. The label is truncated mid-word, the parser tested \code{startswith("REFUSED")}, and so every refusal in every committed run was recorded as an answer.
\end{measured}

All five runs were then re-judged from their stored answers, which costs no generation quota because the answers were already on disk. Two independent implementations --- the throwaway script used during the analysis, and the \code{vidyarag rejudge} command written afterwards --- agree cell for cell.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Profile} & \textbf{Recall (committed)} & \textbf{Recall (corrected)} & \textbf{Precision} & \textbf{FAR}\\
\midrule
\code{baseline} & 0.000 & \textbf{1.000} & 0.667 & 0.130\\
\code{rerank} & 0.000 & \textbf{1.000} & 0.706 & 0.109\\
\code{decompose} & 0.000 & \textbf{1.000} & 0.600 & 0.174\\
\code{corrective} & 1.000 & 1.000 & 0.667 & 0.130\\
\code{guarded} & 0.917 & 0.917 & 0.647 & 0.130\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two \code{guarded} questions could not be re-judged --- the free tier returned 429 on both attempts --- and are recorded as \code{unmeasured} rather than counted as answers.

\begin{keyidea}[the claim this project can actually defend]
The self-check did not create the ability to refuse. Told to answer only from the supplied passages, the baseline already declined when they did not support an answer --- in prose, which is exactly what a broken label reader could not see. On this gold set abstention recall is at ceiling for every profile, and the self-check does not beat plain reranking on abstention precision.

What the loop contributes is the \emph{form} of the refusal: an explicit flag with no citations attached, a claim-level groundedness score, and a retry aimed at the claim that failed. That is a smaller claim than the one the README used to make, and it is the true one. Chapter~\ref{ch:resume} should be read with this substitution applied throughout.
\end{keyidea}

The committed run files still carry the old latency and token figures: restating those needs a full re-run of two profiles, which the free-tier quota did not allow. The README says so rather than quietly leaving the numbers in place.

\section{Two faults found while fixing the first ones}

\begin{finding}[a cached failure is worse than no cache]
The first re-judging run cached every verdict, including failures. Thirty questions hit a 429 at too high a request rate, those failures were written to disk as verdicts, and the next run read them back and never retried --- turning a transient rate limit into a permanent hole in the metric. That is the same shape as F1 one layer down: a failure that quietly becomes a number. Only measured verdicts are cached now, and the 30 poisoned entries were deleted and re-judged \tagM.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[one of the two refusal sentinels was unknown to the detector]
\code{is_structural_abstention} recognised \code{NO_CONTEXT_ANSWER} but not the corrective loop's own \code{ABSTENTION_TEXT}, so re-reading a committed run would pay a model to classify a fixed string the repository already knows verbatim. Found by a test written for the re-judging command, not by reading the code \tagV.
\end{finding}

\section{What a reader should take from this}

\begin{interview}
The strongest thing to say about this project is no longer \enquote{it abstains}. It is: \emph{I built the evaluation, the evaluation lied to me in the direction I wanted, and I caught it.} The bug was invisible because its output was exactly what everyone expected --- 0.000 for pipelines with no abstention mechanism --- and it lived behind a network call that no test could reach. The fix is three lines of parsing and a page of tests that compare the judge's output against known labels without a network.

That is a measurement-integrity story, and it is rarer and more valuable in an interview than a good number. It also comes with a demonstrable habit: the corrected figures are reproducible with one command, and the README states what it retracted rather than quietly replacing it.
\end{interview}
