\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter reports what the five committed evaluation runs measured, what can and cannot be concluded from them, and what the corrected abstention measurement made during this analysis shows. Every committed number below was recomputed from the run files; none is copied from the README.

\section{How to read these numbers}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Each profile was run once}, on 58 questions (46 answerable, 12 unanswerable). There are no confidence intervals.
  \item \textbf{Deterministic retrieval metrics are exact.} Recall, hit rate and MRR are computed from chunk ids; retrieval itself is deterministic, so any difference between profiles is real.
  \item \textbf{Model-judged metrics are noisy.} \code{docs/EVALUATION.md} compares two runs of the same baseline profile: faithfulness 0.954 against 0.948, answer relevancy 0.798 against 0.755, with retrieval-based numbers identical. It concludes that only differences larger than about $\pm$0.05 in answer-dependent metrics are claimable. The earlier of those two runs is not committed.
  \item \textbf{Three committed quantities are wrong} for reasons explained in Chapters~\ref{ch:observability} and~\ref{ch:evaluation}: abstention figures (judge bug), latency of the self-check profiles (double count) and cost of the self-check and decomposition profiles (unrecorded calls). Affected cells are marked.
\end{itemize}

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The sanity check holds.} Reranking reorders the same 20 candidates, so pool recall and hit rate must not change --- and they did not.
  \item \textbf{Reranking moved the right passages up.} Overall recall@context rose from 0.880 to 0.913 and MRR from 0.770 to 0.830. The gap between pool recall (0.967) and context recall shrank from 8.7 to 5.4 points, about 38\% recovered.
  \item \textbf{It helped factual questions and slightly hurt multi-hop ones}: factual recall@context +0.071, multi-hop $-$0.028. A one-question change on 18 multi-hop questions with two gold passages is 0.028, so the multi-hop drop is a single passage.
  \item \textbf{It did real work on almost every question}: on average 17.7 of 20 candidates changed position, the top passage changed on 32 of 58 questions, and at least one passage from outside the dense top five entered the context on 55 of 58.
  \item \textbf{It cost latency}: median 1,022\,ms to 3,100\,ms; the mean (6,856\,ms) is dominated by the first question, 44,109\,ms, which included model loading.
\end{itemize}

Generation metrics barely moved: faithfulness 0.948 to 0.950 and relevancy 0.755 to 0.770, both inside the noise band. Context precision rose from 0.732 to 0.792 (+0.060), consistent with better-ordered context, although it is model-judged.

\section{Decomposition: rejected on the numbers}
\label{sec:res-decompose}

Decomposition split 26 of 58 questions --- 6 factual (21\%), 11 multi-hop (61\%) and 9 unanswerable (75\%). It lowered almost everything: recall@20 0.967 $\rightarrow$ 0.957, recall@context 0.880 $\rightarrow$ 0.826, context precision 0.732 $\rightarrow$ 0.690, context recall 0.938 $\rightarrow$ 0.873, answer relevancy 0.755 $\rightarrow$ 0.709.

The multi-hop result is the instructive one. Pool recall reached 1.000 --- every gold passage was among the 20 candidates --- but recall@context fell to 0.778, and on the 11 multi-hop questions that were actually split it was 0.727, against 0.864 for the same questions under \code{baseline}. Rank fusion found the passages and then ordered them out of the prompt (Section~\ref{sec:decompose}). Multi-hop answer relevancy fell to 0.552. The profile was rejected and kept for reproducibility.

\section{Abstention: the corrected picture}
\label{sec:res-corrective}

\subsection{What the committed runs say}

\code{corrective} refused 12 of 12 unanswerable questions and 3 of 46 answerable ones (precision 0.800, recall 1.000, F1 0.889, false-abstention rate 0.065). \code{guarded} refused 11 of 12 and 2 of 46 (0.846, 0.917, 0.880, 0.043). The three earlier profiles show 0 of 12. Section~\ref{sec:judge-bug} explains why those zeros are not measurements.

\paragraph{Which answerable questions were counted as refused.} The same few questions recur across profiles. In \code{baseline}: \code{fact-002}, \code{fact-024}, \code{multi-003}, \code{multi-006}, \code{multi-007}, \code{multi-013}. In \code{guarded}: \code{fact-002} and \code{multi-015} (structural) plus \code{multi-003}, \code{multi-006}, \code{multi-011}, \code{multi-013}. Several of these are partial answers: they give cited facts and then state what the passages do not cover. For example, \code{baseline} on \code{fact-024}: \enquote{Based on the provided passages, specialized cells in the kidneys found in the juxtaglomerular apparatus secrete renin [2]. The passages do not specify whether these renin-producing cells are located in the afferent arterioles\ldots} The judge's prompt says such answers count as answered, yet the model labelled them refused.

\begin{inferred}[a stricter reading]
Counting only answers that decline outright, the answerable refusals are roughly 1--2 for \code{baseline}, 2 for \code{rerank}, 3--5 for \code{decompose}, 4 for \code{corrective} and 2--3 for \code{guarded}. This classification was made by reading the answers during this analysis, and borderline cases are why ranges are given. The unanswerable column does not depend on it.
\end{inferred}

\subsection{What the self-check actually changed}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Refusing unanswerable questions: nothing.} Every profile refused all 12 (the shipped profile 11). The generator refuses on its own because prompt rule 3 tells it to say what is missing.
  \item \textbf{The form of a refusal: everything.} Without the loop, a refusal is free text of varying wording, may carry citations, and is invisible to any program without a judge. With it, a refusal is one fixed sentence, carries no citations, sets \code{trace.abstained}, and is exactly countable.
  \item \textbf{Partial answers can become full refusals.} \code{multi-011} received a partial answer under \code{rerank} (the hard palate is formed by the maxillary and palatine bones, with the specific origin point missing); under \code{corrective} it received the abstention sentence. When the draft signals a gap, the loop's refusal flag can discard the useful part.
  \item \textbf{The shipped profile missed one refusal}: \code{unans-011}, where the answer declined the catch-muscle comparison and then described latch-state smooth muscle.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Latency and cost}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Means include model loading.} In each run the first question is among the slowest: 44,109\,ms (\code{rerank}), 39,047\,ms (\code{corrective}) and 40,384\,ms (\code{guarded}). Medians are the better summary.
  \item \textbf{Self-check latency is overstated.} The true \code{guarded} mean cannot be recovered from the run file; it is probably around 15 to 19 seconds rather than 21.7 (Section~\ref{sec:latency-bug}).
  \item \textbf{Self-check cost is understated.} Each graded question makes a grading call with about as many input tokens as generation and, in the one call measured, about four times the output tokens. The true list price per question for \code{guarded} is plausibly more than double the reported \$0.00027 \tagI.
  \item \textbf{Actual spend was zero}: all runs used Gemini's free tier.
\end{itemize}

\begin{interview}
If someone asks \enquote{what were your results?}, lead with the measurements that survive scrutiny: reranking lifted context recall from 0.880 to 0.913 and MRR from 0.770 to 0.830 on an unchanged candidate pool; decomposition was measured and rejected because it dropped multi-hop context recall to 0.778. For the self-check, say what it really does --- turns the generator's inconsistent prose refusals into a single, citation-free, countable abstention --- and, if you choose to, that you found and quantified a scoring bug that had made the baseline look unable to refuse. That sentence demonstrates more evaluation maturity than the original headline did.
\end{interview}
